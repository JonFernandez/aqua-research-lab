Island Grid Intelligence

AI forecasting and classical and quantum optimization benchmarks for off-grid Philippine microgrids.

In brief

Many small Philippine islands are not connected to the main power grids and rely heavily on diesel generators. Hybrid systems with solar panels and batteries could help, and better scheduling may increase that benefit: predicting demand and sunshine, and deciding when to charge, discharge or start the generator. This concept would develop AI forecasts for that scheduling problem and compare classical and quantum optimization methods for it, in simulation and on a small laboratory testbed. It is not a product for a live grid.

The real-world problem

On an island microgrid, poor forecasts and simple scheduling rules can waste solar power, wear out batteries early and keep diesel generators running longer than needed. Energy management is therefore central to whether hybrid systems deliver what they promise.

Scheduling these systems is a combinatorial problem: which generator runs when, and how hard to charge or discharge storage, under uncertainty. Problems of this shape can be written in the form that quantum annealers and some gate-based quantum algorithms accept. Whether that helps on realistic Philippine instances is unknown, and worth testing carefully.

The scientific questions

How accurately can deep learning forecast short-horizon load and solar generation for small, weather-variable island microgrids, and what representation of uncertainty best supports robust scheduling?

On realistic island scheduling problems, how do quantum annealing and QAOA compare with classical mixed-integer programming on solution quality, run time and scaling, and at what problem size, if any, does the quantum formulation become competitive?

What difference does an integrated forecast-plus-optimization system make to fuel use, renewable use, battery wear and unmet demand, compared with rule-based scheduling, when tested on a bench-scale testbed?

Proposed approach

  • A digital twin. A simulation of a representative island microgrid built from satellite solar data, reference load profiles, public operating data and site visits.
  • Probabilistic forecasts. Sequence models that predict load and solar generation with ranges, so the scheduler can hedge, evaluated against simple persistence and statistical baselines.
  • A fair optimization benchmark. The same scheduling problems solved by classical mixed-integer programming and by quantum methods on simulators and freely available cloud hardware.
  • Classical by default. The working scheduler uses the classical solver. The quantum comparison is a research result, whatever it shows.
  • Hardware-in-the-loop testing. A small bench testbed with a solar panel, battery and programmable load, running against the digital twin.
Methods in more detail, for specialists

Forecasting. Temporal convolutional and transformer-based models for day-ahead and intra-day load and solar forecasts, with quantile or distributional outputs.

Formulation. Unit commitment and dispatch (diesel on/off scheduling, battery charge and discharge, load prioritization) written as QUBO/Ising problems.

Solvers. Simulated and quantum annealing and QAOA on simulator backends, with free-tier quantum cloud runs where available, benchmarked against mixed-integer linear programming on solution quality, time-to-solution, constraint satisfaction and scaling.

Control loop. A rolling-horizon energy management loop integrating forecasts and optimization, with a modular optimizer interface.

Evaluation. Fuel use, unmet demand, battery cycling and renewable utilization against rule-based dispatch, on the testbed and in simulation.

Intended outputs

  • An open digital twin and dataset for a representative Philippine island microgrid.
  • A published, application-grounded comparison of quantum and classical optimization for island scheduling.
  • A modular energy-management research prototype, tested on a bench-scale testbed.
  • Open code and peer-reviewed publications.
Who might benefit
  • Power-systems and energy researchers, through an open island microgrid model and benchmark.
  • Electric cooperatives and off-grid operators, through evidence on which forecasting and scheduling approaches are worth piloting.
  • Quantum computing researchers, through realistic benchmark problems with a strong classical comparison.

Status and limitations

  • This is a research concept. A power-systems co-investigator and a site partner are not yet confirmed.
  • Evaluation would be in simulation and on a bench-scale testbed only. Field deployment, safety certification, regulatory approval and utility system integration are out of scope.
  • Quantum methods are not expected to outperform mature classical solvers on current hardware. No quantum advantage is assumed.
  • The digital twin would represent one representative island system; generalizing to other islands is future work.
