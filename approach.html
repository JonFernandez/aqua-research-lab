Approach

Good research is mostly about what you refuse to assume

We do not assume that more data helps, that a newer model is better, or that a quantum method beats a classical one. Each study is designed so the evidence can say no.

The research path

  1. A real problem

    Stated precisely enough to be tested.

  2. Data, design and domain knowledge

    Open data, field measurement or a carefully designed experiment, shaped with specialists.

  3. Models and fair benchmarks

    Comparisons, baselines and success criteria fixed before fitting.

  4. Honest results

    Reported with uncertainty and evidence type, including "it did not help".

  5. Possible application

    Prototypes for partners to test, with their limits stated.

Conceptual workflow. Application comes last and is never assumed.

Design commitments

Commitments in how we design studies, not claims about results.

Say what kind of evidence it is

Simulation, model output and human data answer different questions.

Example

Our committee study simulates a planned experiment to test whether the design works; it is not evidence about how real committees behave. Our AI-respondent study compares model outputs with historical human results, not with a new human sample.

Fair baselines, decided in advance

Same data, same validation, criteria fixed before fitting.

Example

In the bamboo proposal, the only difference between the baseline and the full model is the extra satellite inputs, so any gain can be attributed to them. In the committee study, model recovery and precision targets were fixed before the simulation was run.

Uncertainty from every source

Intervals reported, and checked against their claimed coverage.

Example

The committee study reports that each effect was estimated less precisely than planned (±0.10–0.22 against ±0.05) and estimates how much larger a study would need to be.

Validation that respects structure

Hold out what a model has not seen: places, committees or events.

Example

Nearby locations look alike, so random splits flatter a model. Where locations are correlated, our designs use spatially blocked validation; the committee simulation tests model recovery on held-out committees.

Decision points and honest rescoping

If the data fall short, the claims narrow rather than stretch.

Null results are results

"It didn't help" is reported with the same care as a positive finding.

Quantum, two ways

Quantum computing and quantum probability are different things

Quantum computing

Algorithms for quantum hardware. We are exploring quantum algorithms, quantum optimization, and error mitigation: algorithmic ways to reduce the effect of noise on today's devices. Methods are tested on simulators, and on freely available cloud hardware where useful, against mature classical methods. Where a working system is needed, the classical method is the default.

Quantum probability

A mathematical toolkit for judgment. It uses the vectors and projections of quantum theory to model effects such as question order. It borrows the mathematics, not the physics, and makes no claim that brains or language models are quantum systems.

We will not

  • Claim a quantum advantage without a fair benchmark that shows one.
  • Choose a weak classical baseline to make a quantum method look better.
  • Present a quantum-probability fit as evidence of physical quantum processes.
  • Describe simulator results as if they ran on large quantum hardware.

Open science

How we share our work

Posters are linked from each research page. Code and protocol for the committee study are available from the authors on request. Datasets and code for future projects are designed for open release where permissions allow.

What open release would include
  • Versioned datasets with documentation of collection and processing.
  • Code and trained models, with instructions to rerun the analysis.
  • Exact field locations and anything identifying landholders or participants kept access-controlled.
  • A pre-publication check on whether anything needs protection; the expected outcome is open release.

Responsible use

What our research outputs are not

  • Not a substitute for people. Our work tests whether AI respondents behave like humans; it does not show that they can replace them.
  • Not a hazard warning service. In an emergency, follow PAGASA, PHIVOLCS and your local disaster office.
  • Not carbon credits. Bamboo estimates would be prototypes for expert review; no carbon income is promised.
  • Not a deployed grid controller. Microgrid work would stay in simulation and on a lab testbed.

People and places

Partners, not data sources

Field partners help shape the work and get something back from it. Studies with people begin only after ethics review, and field permissions are asked for one by one.
