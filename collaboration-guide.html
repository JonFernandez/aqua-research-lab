How collaborations are arranged

The practical details, for when a conversation turns into a project. Specific arrangements are agreed case by case, under each institution's own requirements.

Authorship and credit

Credit follows documented contribution, not institutional affiliation alone. We discuss authorship early and decide author order by contribution rather than assigning it in advance.

Data and publication

Before shared data collection begins, the partners settle in writing who holds the data, what each side may publish, and who approves outside requests, following each institution's requirements. Each institution keeps its own research-use rights. Our default is open release of methods and code where permissions allow, with sensitive information kept access-controlled.

Research with people

Studies with human participants, such as a pilot with real committees or a matched human survey sample, would begin only after the appropriate research-ethics review. No recruitment is under way.

Fieldwork and permissions

Our most developed field proposal shows how we intend to work with field partners:

  • Partners help design the protocol, choose sites and interpret results, and receive the cleaned data and maps for their own use.
  • Each permission is asked for separately: entering land, flying a drone, taking images, publishing maps and releasing data.
  • Consent comes from whoever has the right to give it, whether land is privately owned, tenanted, communally managed or under government jurisdiction.
  • Benefits are named plainly: maps, plain-language reports, training, and compensation for time and field assistance.

These describe that proposal's design. The terms of any actual partnership would be agreed with the partner.

What we will not promise

  • Income from carbon markets. Bamboo carbon estimates would be research prototypes, not certified credits.
  • Official hazard warnings. Those come from PAGASA, PHIVOLCS and other mandated agencies.
  • A replacement for a utility's or agency's own systems.
  • That a quantum method will outperform a classical one. That is a question we test.
